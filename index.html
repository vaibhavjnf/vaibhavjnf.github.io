About

I pursue cybersecurity from my own research lab (RL), not a classroom. Leads are mapped from published source, verified locally on my own systems and accounts, and logged — including the clean ones. Most leads turn out clean; working through them anyway is the discipline.

My main corner is Android & device security. I build in the Android / scrcpy ecosystem, so I research it too: device tooling, pairing and session models, and the trust boundaries between a phone, a host PC, and an AI agent. I also review the open-source code I depend on — anything real goes privately to the maintainer first, with a proposed fix, never a public post before the vendor publishes.

Alongside research, I am a builder. I have been building on GitHub since 2015 — around 114 repositories of AI tools, 3D editors, and voice-first systems under Vibin. And I run real systems: I am the third-generation owner-operator of Jodhpur Namkeen in Kota, a family business running since 1971. Operating a live business every day is a different kind of security education — you learn what downtime, fraud, and a broken integration actually cost.

Credits, when they come, are listed on my security page with a link to the vendor's own page — evidence, not claims.

Projects

  1. AB

    Android Bridge ↗

    A consent-first Android bridge, built in public. The owner hosts a dashboard on their own PC; the phone's companion app binds only to that PC's Tailscale identity (pinned at pairing, by QR). An AI agent can request the screen (via scrcpy) or SMS only through scoped, time-boxed, audit-logged tools — owner-approve by default, revocable in one tap, no public-internet listener out of the box. The security model was designed first, because a tool that can see a phone screen has to earn that access.
